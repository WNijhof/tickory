The screen: sidebar and task list

The sidebar on the left is for choosing what you see, the task list is on the right. This page explains where everything is and what the circles, numbers and chips mean.

The screen with Today. The sidebar is on the left, the list on the right. Above the list is the input field for adding a task quickly.

A row in the list

Every task is one row:

  • The circle on the left is the button to tick the task off. The color of its edge is the priority: red for P1, orange for P2, blue for P3 and grey for P4 (none).
  • The name of the task, and below it in grey the first line of the description, if there is one. Click the name or the row to open the task; see The task window.
  • Chips below that, in this order: the date (its color shows whether it is overdue, today or later; for a recurrence there is a ↻ in front), the deadline, the number of completed subtasks (“1/4”), the number of comments, the labels (@name, in the color of the label), a bell 🔔 if a reminder is going to go off, and who the task is for (“for Bram”, “for you”) in a shared project.
  • The project is on the right, with its color, in Today and Upcoming. If the task is in a section, that is shown too (“Huishouden / Klusjes”).

Subtasks in the list

Subtasks are indented below their task. If a task has subtasks, the “1/4” chip shows a small arrow: click it to hide or show the subtasks. The app remembers that too. You make subtasks in the task window.

Ticking off and bringing back

You can undo every change you make on purpose with the button in the message or with Ctrl+Z: see Undo. Picking and editing several tasks at once is covered in Selecting several tasks.

Click the circle to tick a task off. A moment later it disappears from the list and for a few seconds the message appears at the bottom, with the button .

  • Viewing completed tasks: everything you ticked off is listed per day under in the sidebar (see Completed). At the bottom of a project is also (with the number). Click the circle of a completed task to reopen it. If you reopen a task with subtasks, the subtasks stay ticked until you reopen them yourself.
  • Recurring tasks do not disappear when you tick them off: the date moves on to the next time. See ticking off recurrences.
  • If you tick off a task with subtasks, its subtasks are ticked off too.

Today and Upcoming

  • shows all open tasks dated today, from all your projects, and above them whatever is . At the top you see how many tasks you completed today against your daily goal (see Productivity). moves all overdue tasks to another day at once (the time and any recurrence stay; you can undo it). If there is nothing to do you see “All done for today”.
  • shows the next fourteen days, starting tomorrow, with a heading per day. Days without tasks are skipped.

Within a day, tasks without a time come first, then those with a time, from early to late; at the same time the highest priority goes first. In a shared project you also see the tasks assigned to someone else here. Tasks without a date are in neither view; you find those in their project.

Moving tasks and projects

You drag tasks and projects to their place. A line shows where it will end up. Drop a task on the top edge or bottom edge of another task and it goes before or after it; drop it in the middle of a task and it becomes a subtask of that task (the frame around that task shows this). Subtasks always move along with their task.

  • Tasks: change the order within a project, move to another section (also onto the heading of a section, which puts it at the top), make a subtask, and drag a subtask out again. Drop a task on a project in the sidebar and it moves there; the message below has . In a shared project every member can move tasks.
  • Projects in the sidebar: change the order, drop a project on another project (it becomes a subproject) and drop a subproject on the heading (it becomes an ordinary project again). Only the owner of the project can do this; members do not see it as an option. The Inbox stays where it is. You can order favourites among themselves, as long as they are projects with the same parent.
  • With the mouse: take hold of a row and drag; it starts after a few millimetres. An ordinary click still opens the task. Esc cancels the drag, and at the top or bottom edge of the screen the list scrolls along by itself.
  • On a phone: press and hold a row for a moment and then drag, or take the handle (the six dots) on the right of a task, which drags at once. Swiping just scrolls. To move a task to another project, drag it against the left edge of the screen: the screen opens and you drop it on the project.
  • With the keyboard: Alt+↑ and Alt+↓ move the task or project you are on one place up or down (at the edge of a section a task goes to the previous or next section). Alt+→ puts it under the task or project above; Alt+← takes it out again. A screen reader announces where it ended up, or why it could not.

Moving also works without a connection and is sent later. If something has changed in the meantime so that it is no longer possible (a section was removed, a task deleted), the change is undone and you see why. In and tasks are sorted by date and priority; there you can only drag them to a project in the sidebar.

The status in the top right

The top right shows whether everything is up to date (), whether changes are waiting, or whether the server cannot be reached. Click it to sync right away. See Working offline.

What is not possible yet
