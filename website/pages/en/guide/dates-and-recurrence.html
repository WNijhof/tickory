Dates and recurrences

You type a date the way you would say it, in Dutch. The app recognises it in the line you add a task with, and in the field when you edit a task.

Every example on this page was checked against the app's own date parser. They assume Tuesday 17 November 2026, 10 in the morning; on another day the resulting dates are of course different.

Good to know

The app is bilingual, but the date language is Dutch for now, also when you set the interface to English. English input such as tomorrow is not recognised; English date language comes later. Until then, this page gives the Dutch phrases with their meaning. What works in any language: 31-12, 31-12-2026 and, in the field, 2026-12-31 and 2026-12-31T14:30.

A date without a year is always the next occurrence: on 17 November 2026, 16 november therefore means 16 November 2027. A date in the past, such as gisteren (yesterday), is not recognised.

Mind the hours 1 to 7

A whole hour from 1 to 7 without anything else is read as afternoon or evening, the way it is usually meant in Dutch: morgen om 7 uur is 19:00. If you mean the early morning, type morgen om 07:00 or “7 uur 's ochtends”. This does not happen for 8 to 12 o'clock: morgen om 8 uur is 8:00.

Holidays

A (Dutch) holiday becomes a date when there is a preposition in front of it: voor (before), tegen (towards), op (on), uiterlijk (at the latest) or per (as of). Without a preposition it stays plain text, so that “Kerst inkopen doen” (do the Christmas shopping) remains a task and gets no date. The date is always the next occurrence; Easter and the days that depend on it are calculated for each year.

Recurrences

A task that comes back starts with elke (every; also elk, iedere, ieder), with om de (every other), or is a single word such as dagelijks (daily). In the list you recognise a recurrence by the ↻ sign in front of the date. The tables show the first time the task is due.

For a day that does not exist in every month, the app moves to the last day of that month and back again afterwards: “elke maand op de 31e” (every month on the 31st) gives 31 January, 28 February, 31 March.

What happens when you tick off a recurrence

A recurring task does not disappear when you tick it off: the date moves on to the next occurrence and the task stays open. A message at the bottom tells you when that is (“Next due: …”). The next occurrence is counted from the date the task had, not from the day you tick it off. If you left a daily task for three days, ticking it off moves it one day and it is still listed as overdue; tick it off again until the date is right, or type a new date in the edit window.

In a line with a task

When adding a task, the app takes the date out of the line; what remains is the name of the task. The date can be at the start, at the end or in the middle.

What deliberately does not become a date

Better no date than a wrong one. That is why the app leaves these lines alone:

You typeWhy no date is set
Kerst inkopen doenA holiday without a preposition is just a word.
Mei bellenA bare month name (mei, May) can also be someone's name.
Van 1 mei tot 5 mei wegTwo dates in one line: the app does not guess which one you mean.
Hoofdstuk 3A number after a word such as hoofdstuk (chapter), pagina (page) or versie (version) is not a day.
Zo snel mogelijk“As soon as possible”: no day can be determined.

If a date is recognised where you do not want one, put a ~ in front of it; see switching date recognition off.

What is not possible

  • elke vijfde dinsdag (every fifth Tuesday): a fifth Tuesday does not exist in every month. Use elke laatste dinsdag (every last Tuesday).
  • elke tweede week van de maand (every second week of the month), and recurrences with an end date or a maximum number of times.
  • A recurrence “so many days after I ticked the task off”: the next occurrence always depends on the previous date.
  • Dates in the past, and English or other languages.

The Due date field in the task window

Click a task and you find the field . There you type the same language as above. Right below the field the app shows what it makes of it (Will be: Thu 26 Nov), so that with a phrase like “volgende week donderdag” you can see which day it will be. If the app does not recognise the text, it says so and the task is not saved until you correct it. Empty the field to remove the date. If the task already has a date, the English interface shows it in the field as 2026-11-17T17:30 (a recurrence as you typed it); the Dutch interface shows 17-11-2026 om 17:30.

Below the field are quick choices too (, , , , ); see The task window. The deadline is chosen with your browser's date picker.

How dates are shown in the list

  • today, tomorrow and yesterday are shown as words; a day within a week as a weekday (“Friday”); further away as “Thu 26 Nov”, and with the year added in another year. (In the Dutch interface: vandaag, morgen, gisteren, “vrijdag”, “do 26 nov”.)
  • A time follows it: “tomorrow 09:00”. The app uses a 24-hour clock in both languages.
  • A date that has passed turns red, and from the day after the task is listed in under . A task with a time already turns red as soon as that time has passed, but stays among today's tasks for the rest of that day.
