Reminders

A reminder tells you at an agreed moment that a task is due. You set it on a task; the notification arrives in your browser or in the Android app.

In short

  • A task with a time gets a reminder by itself, 30 minutes before by default. You can change that, or switch it off.
  • You can add reminders yourself: at the time of the task, from 10 minutes to 3 days before, or at a date and time of your own.
  • Reminders are personal: even in a shared project nobody sees yours.
  • A notification arrives on your device if you have turned on notifications in the browser or use the Android app. Without that you only see the bell 🔔 in the list.

A reminder on a task

Click a task. In the window there is a part with your reminders on this task.

Three reminders on the task “Tandarts bellen voor controle” (due tomorrow at 9). Each reminder shows when it goes off.
  1. Under choose when: , 10, 30 or 45 minutes before, 1, 2 or 3 hours before, 1, 2 or 3 days before, or for a moment of your own.
  2. If you choose a moment of your own, fill in the date and the time.
  3. Choose .

You delete a reminder with the × button after its line. This takes effect immediately, just like adding subtasks: you do not have to click , and does not undo it. If you add exactly the same reminder again, nothing happens.

Two kinds, and a third

Before ()
Tied to the time of the task. If you move the task to another time, the reminder moves with it. This works only on a task with a time, for example “tomorrow at 2 pm”. On a task with only a day, the reminder shows : it does nothing yet and goes off as soon as the task gets a time.
A date and time of your own, independent of the task. Also on a task without a date.
A reminder when you arrive at or leave a place, such as Home or the supermarket. It has no time and only works in the Android app, based on the position of your phone, which never leaves the device. See Reminders at a place.

The lines are in the order they go off; whatever does nothing yet is at the bottom. If you remove the time from a task, your reminders before it disappear (a reminder at a set time stays). If you later give a task without any reminder a time, the automatic reminder is added.

The bell

A task for which a reminder is going to go off has a bell 🔔 in the list. If a task only has a reminder before it but no time yet, there is no bell: nothing will go off. A completed task no longer gives notifications.

The automatic reminder

If you add a task with a time and do not give a reminder yourself, it gets one by itself: 30 minutes before by default. The same applies when you give an existing task a time for the first time and it had no reminder yet.

  1. Click your name at the bottom of the sidebar () and choose .
  2. Under pick an option from the list: at the time of the task, 10 minutes to 3 days before, or .
The settings for reminders. Below these, “Notifications on this device” shows what still has to be done on this device; see below.

This changes nothing about reminders that already exist. It applies to tasks you add or give a time afterwards.

The time zone

A time on a task is a clock time: “9 o'clock” is 9 o'clock wherever you are. Notifications that the server sends are converted using the time zone in your settings.

  • The app records your device's time zone by itself the first time you open it, if none has been set.
  • In , under , you see which time zone applies. If your device differs (you moved or are travelling), there is a button to use this device's time zone.
  • If a user has no time zone yet, the server uses its default time zone (DEFAULT_TIMEZONE, see for administrators).
  • The Android app schedules reminders by the clock of the phone itself.

Personal, and in shared projects

  • Every user has their own reminders. If you set a reminder on a task in a shared project, only you see and get it.
  • It does not matter who the task is assigned to: you can set a reminder on any task you can see.
  • If you leave a project, or the owner removes you, your reminders on those tasks lapse.
  • If a task is completed or deleted (by you or by someone else in the project), its reminders no longer go off.

Recurring tasks

For a recurring task a reminder before the time goes off every time: when you tick the task off, its date moves to the next occurrence and the reminder moves with it. “Every weekday at 8:30” with 30 minutes before therefore gives a notification every weekday at 8:00. A reminder at a set time goes off once; it does not move along.

Notifications

Without a notification you only see a reminder when you open the app. How the notification reaches you depends on the device. Notifications from shared projects (someone assigns you a task, mentions you, comments) take the same route; which kinds you want to get that way is chosen in the same screen under . In the browser

Your browser can show a notification even when Tickory is not open. The server sends it through your browser's push service (Web Push). What you need:

  • Your Tickory is reachable over HTTPS (or you use localhost). See HTTPS.
  • A browser with push notifications. On an iPhone or iPad it only works if you first add Tickory to the home screen and open the app from there.

  3. Choose . If a notification arrives, it works. If not, the window says what went wrong.

What this does and does not guarantee

Sending has been tested with a simulated push service. Whether a notification arrives on your device, and whether that also happens when the browser is closed, depends on your browser, your operating system and their push service (Google, Mozilla, Microsoft or Apple). So use , and do not rely on it if it proves unreliable on your device: the Android app schedules the reminders itself and does not depend on that.

In the Android app

The Android app fetches your reminders from your server and sets them as alarms on the phone itself. That is why they also go off when your phone has no internet. The app does not need a push service, so no HTTPS for the notifications either. What still has to be allowed is shown in , under .

How late is too late

The server checks every 20 seconds which reminders are due, so a notification arrives within about half a minute of the moment. If the server was down for a while, or a notification could not be delivered, it is still sent as long as it is at most an hour late; anything older lapses. The same reminder at the same moment arrives only once. The Android app shows what is at most an hour late in the same way.

For administrators

  • There is nothing to install. On first start the server creates its own keys for notifications and keeps them in the database. A backup therefore contains them; if you start with an empty database, they are new keys and every device registers again by itself when the app is opened.
  • VAPID_SUBJECT in .env is your contact address for the push service, mailto:you@example.org or an https address. Leaving it empty is allowed: no address is sent then. Apple's push service (Safari, iPhone, iPad) does ask for one. There is deliberately no default, so that the project's address is not sent on behalf of every installation. An invalid value is ignored, with a warning in the log.
  • DEFAULT_TIMEZONE is the time zone for users who have not set one themselves; Europe/Amsterdam by default.
  • The server itself must be able to reach the outside: it connects to the push services of Google, Mozilla, Microsoft and Apple (only those; over https). If you have restricted outgoing traffic, allow those.
  • A device registers with a session. If that session is revoked or the account is blocked, that device gets no more notifications. At most 20 devices per user are registered; the oldest lapse.
  • Notifications in the browser only work over HTTPS; that does not apply to the Android app.
# .env
VAPID_SUBJECT=mailto:you@example.org
DEFAULT_TIMEZONE=Europe/Amsterdam
